## Abandoned Lime Scooters Begin Annual Migration South for the Winter

As temperatures dip below 50 degrees, East Lakeview's population of abandoned Lime scooters has begun its long journey south, local naturalists confirmed Tuesday.

"You see them gathering on the corners first," said Denise Harrow, a Melrose Street resident who has tracked the herd for three seasons. "One on its side in front of a hydrant. Then two blocking a ramp. By Friday there's a whole flock lying across the sidewalk at Belmont, waiting for the right wind."

Experts believe the scooters navigate by instinct, steering clear of every designated parking spot along the route. Most are expected to winter in Lincoln Park and return in April, rested and fully discharged.

Residents are urged not to disturb the scooters during migration, which they say is no change from the rest of the year.

## Tilly's Bagel Line Reaches Skokie

The line for Saturday bagels at Tilly's extended past the city limits this weekend, with the last customer reporting that she was "technically in Skokie, but making good progress."

"I got in line in October," said Mark Delgado, who holds position 4,112. "My kids were in strollers when I started. Now they're asking about colleges."

Line volunteers have set up water stations every half mile, and a pop-up Little Free Library near Devon is lending books to anyone who expects to wait more than one season.

When asked whether the opening of a new bagel shop nearby might shorten the wait, several people in line looked confused and said they would check after they got their bagel.

## Stickered Garbage Can on Broadway Added to Chicago Historical Registry

As West Lakeview debates landmarking its greystones, East Lakeview quietly secured protected status for its own architectural treasure: the stickered garbage can on Broadway.

"This can tells the story of our neighborhood," said a spokesperson for the Chicago Historical Registry, gesturing at an estimated 3,400 layered stickers for bands, bars and causes. "Archaeologists believe the bottom layer dates to the Daley administration. One sticker is just the word 'no.'"

Under the new designation, all future stickers must be approved by a review board and applied in a historically appropriate style. Emptying the can now requires a permit.

"Honestly, it's the most beautiful thing on the block," said one longtime resident. "Besides the other garbage can."

*Caption: The can, photographed in its natural state, has not been fully visible since 2011.*

## Temporary Construction Fence on Broadway Granted Permanent Residency

After years of faithful service, a temporary chain link construction fence on Broadway was officially recognized Thursday as a permanent member of the community.

"Some of us have lived here less time than this fence," said Priya Nair, a Briar Place resident. "It's seen three businesses open and close. It's earned its place."

The fence, which originally arrived for a project no neighbor can remember, has become a fixture of daily life, serving as a bike rack, lost glove display and community bulletin board. Neighbors say the ceremony was moving, though nobody was able to reach the stage because it was behind the fence.

City officials say the fence will be removed when the project is complete, a date they described as "soon, in a geological sense."

## Merlot Library Uncorks Its Newest Vintage

Election season brought crowds not seen since the last free tote bag giveaway to Merlo Library on Belmont, where early voters praised the bold civic finish of this year's vintage.

"Notes of democracy, a hint of overdue fines and a long finish from the line," said one voter, swirling a paper "I Voted" sticker under her nose. "Very drinkable."

Library staff gently reminded patrons that there is no wine, that there has never been wine and that the name has always been Merlo. Several voters nodded, then asked if the library offered a flight.

The library recommends pairing your ballot with a quiet afternoon and a book that is not due for three weeks.

*Caption: Voters wait to sample the 2026 vintage, which critics call "full bodied and fully accessible."*
